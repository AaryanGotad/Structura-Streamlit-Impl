Why might Structura get a line wrong?

Structura is designed for English medical and clinical research abstracts, especially those with the rhetorical patterns found in its training data. It always assigns one of five labels, even when the text is outside that domain, so predictions should be treated as helpful signals rather than definitive answers.

  • Some sentences naturally contain information belonging to multiple categories, such as a method and a result in the same sentence.
  • Not all scientific abstracts follow a rigid, predictable structure, and sentence boundaries may not match the model's expectations.
  • The model was trained primarily on medical and life-science abstracts, so robotics, news, general prose, fragments, and other out-of-domain text may be classified less reliably.
  • Structura uses lightweight warning signals for likely out-of-domain input, including unusual sentence counts, low average confidence, or frequent changes between predicted classes. These warnings do not change the predictions and are not definitive proof that text is out of domain.
  • Confidence scores are model outputs, not calibrated probabilities of correctness, and the model has no explicit UNKNOWN or MIXED category.

About Structura

Structura is a full-stack machine learning application that analyzes scientific abstracts sentence by sentence. It keeps the original wording and identifies the likely rhetorical role of each sentence, making the flow of a study easier to scan and understand.

  • Each sentence is classified as BACKGROUND, OBJECTIVE, METHODS, RESULTS, or CONCLUSIONS, with alternative predictions and confidence scores available for inspection.
  • The application combines a lightweight HTML, CSS, and JavaScript frontend with a Spring Boot API and a FastAPI/TensorFlow inference service.
  • It works best with English medical and life-science abstracts. Predictions are useful signals rather than definitive answers, especially for mixed sentences or text outside the model's training domain.
